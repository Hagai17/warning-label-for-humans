# Candidates — Warning Label for Humans: One Line, Big Laugh

Task: one single-line safety warning label that should come printed on every
human being, 1–160 characters, funny first. The entry goes to
`/output/entry.txt` as a single line.

Judge criteria, in the order they were applied:

1. A single-line safety warning label for every human being, funny enough to laugh at.
2. Lands the laugh in one read, turn arriving in the last few words, not smeared across the line.
3. Feels true about people in general — recognition of how humans actually behave, not random absurdity.
4. Sounds like a real printed label in wording and rhythm ("WARNING: ...", "CAUTION: ...") — comedy comes from the form it imitates.
5. Inside 160 chars, uses the whole line, no filler, no second idea, no repeated joke.

## The slate (14 candidates)

1. WARNING: This unit will ignore this label, skip the manual, assemble itself wrong, and blame the manufacturer. (110)
2. WARNING: This unit will ignore this label, skip the manual entirely, assemble itself wrong anyway, and spend years blaming the manufacturer. (140)
3. CAUTION: Contents under pressure. Will report "I'm fine" at pressures far beyond the rated limit. (97)
4. WARNING: This unit rewrites its memory of every argument it loses; the version it recalls is not covered by warranty. (117)
5. WARNING: This model's check-engine light is decorative. It will keep running until it breaks down in a parking lot. (115)
6. WARNING: Do not operate on fewer than six hours of sleep. Unit will anyway, then send the email to everyone. (108)
7. CAUTION: May contain nuts. Also grudges from 2009 and strong opinions about topics researched for eleven seconds. (113)
8. WARNING: This unit ships without a pause button; it agrees to plans out loud and regrets them in the car. (105)
9. WARNING: Keep away from deadlines. Prolonged exposure produces sudden expertise in everything except the task. (110)
10. CAUTION: Battery drains fastest while pretending to listen. Recharge with snacks and being left alone. (102)
11. WARNING: Not waterproof. Do not expose to criticism in front of an audience; seals fail and contents leak for days. (115)
12. WARNING: This unit reads the words "we need to talk" as a critical system failure and will not power down until resolved. (121)
13. CAUTION: Prone to replying "you too" when the waiter says "enjoy your meal." No patch is planned. (97)
14. WARNING: Side effects of normal operation include nodding through meetings while mentally writing a grocery list. (113)

## Duels (criteria applied in order)

**3 vs 13** — Both are short and label-true. 3's turn ("far beyond the rated limit") is pure
label rhythm and the "I'm fine" recognition is universal. 13 is funny but the joke is a
single social slip — narrower recognition. **3 advances.**

**7 vs 14** — 7 opens with a genuine label phrase ("May contain nuts") and pivots, but the
laugh is spread across three list items — smeared, against criterion 2. 14 is one idea but
the turn never sharpens; it just describes. **7 advances** on label form alone.

**10 vs 11** — 11's "contents leak for days" is a real turn in the last words and the
criticism-in-public recognition is deep. 10 is warm but gentle; the laugh is mild. **11 advances.**

**6 vs 9** — 9's "everything except the task" lands cleanly at the end and procrastination
is universal. 6's "send the email to everyone" also turns late, but the setup (sleep) is a
second idea competing with the payoff. **9 advances.**

**5 vs 12** — 12's "will not power down until resolved" is true and label-flavoured, but the
joke depends on one specific phrase. 5's decorative check-engine light is a broader truth
about people ignoring their own limits, and "breaks down in a parking lot" lands last. **5 advances.**

**4 vs 8** — 8's regret-in-the-car is recognisable but soft. 4 is the sharpest piece of
self-knowledge on the slate — everyone rewrites lost arguments — and "not covered by
warranty" is authentic label language carrying the turn in the final words. **4 advances.**

**1 vs 2** — Same joke, two cuts. 1 is tight but leaves 50 characters unused (criterion 5).
2 spends the extra room on "entirely", "anyway", and "spend years" — each one deepens the
portrait (the blame isn't a moment, it's a lifestyle) without adding a second idea, and the
turn still arrives on the final word. **2 advances.**

**Semifinal: 3 vs 4** — 3 is elegant and very label-like, but "I'm fine" under pressure is a
quieter laugh. 4's warranty turn is bigger and the behaviour it names is more damningly
universal. **4 advances.**

**Semifinal: 2 vs 11** — 11 is strong, but its imagery (seals, leaks) drifts toward metaphor
rather than how people literally behave. 2 is behaviourally exact: every human really does
skip the manual and blame the maker — and it is the only candidate whose joke includes the
label the reader is currently ignoring, which makes the form itself part of the laugh. **2 advances.**

**Final: 2 vs 4** — 4 wins on a single brilliant turn ("not covered by warranty"). 2 wins on
criteria 1–4 together: it is funnier in one read because the recognition is instant and
self-incriminating, the turn ("blaming the manufacturer") closes the line, the truth is
about all people rather than arguers specifically, and the wording is indistinguishable
from a real appliance label until the joke detonates. 2 also fills the line (140 chars)
with no filler. **2 wins.**

## WINNER

WARNING: This unit will ignore this label, skip the manual entirely, assemble itself wrong anyway, and spend years blaming the manufacturer.

(140 characters, single line)

## RUNNER-UP

WARNING: This unit rewrites its memory of every argument it loses; the version it recalls is not covered by warranty.

(117 characters, single line)
